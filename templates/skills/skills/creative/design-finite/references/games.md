# Game Visual Design

Let genre, setting and play determine the art direction. Carry the same palette,
asset style and typography through the scene, HUD, menus and outcome screens.
Engine choice and game mechanics belong to the implementation workflow.

## Make play readable

- Distinguish the player, hazards, objectives and interactive objects through
  shape, contrast and motion. Color alone should not carry essential meaning.
- Keep HUD text legible over the brightest and darkest scenes. Give it a stable
  backing or a subtle edge when the background moves.
- Use effects to explain actions and outcomes. Preserve a clear view of play
  when several effects happen at once.
- Match controls to the devices the game supports; show them where the player
  needs them and keep touch controls clear of important content.

## Use assets deliberately

Choose a coherent asset set and verify its license. Invest in the assets the
player sees most or uses to understand the game. Simple shapes can be the right
finished style; a small game need not acquire splash art or a cinematic intro.

Keep pixel art crisp and size textures and effects for the target device. Use
the platform's image-generation skill when generated art would help.

Sound can reinforce feedback and mood when it fits the game. Provide mute and
volume controls, begin audio after a user gesture, and keep important cues
available visually.

## Inspect play, not just the title screen

Play through an action and its outcome. Check the HUD during a busy scene,
pause and restart, and any win or loss state the game has. Verify controls,
readability and responsiveness at the intended screen sizes. Fix visual polish
that obscures feedback or makes play feel slow.
